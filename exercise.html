Q1 My father went to a shop from home which is located at a distance of 250 m on a straight road. On reaching there, he discovered that he forgot to carry a cloth bag. He came home to take it, went to the shop again, bought provisions and came back home. How much was the total distance travelled by him? What was his displacement from home?Very short answer

Home → shop : 250 m. Shop → home : 250 m. Home → shop : 250 m. Shop → home : 250 m.

Total distance = 250 + 250 + 250 + 250 = 1000 m = 1 km.

He finishes at home, exactly where he started, so his displacement = 0 m.

Q2 A student runs from the ground floor to the fourth floor of a school building to collect a book and then comes down to their classroom on the second floor. If the height of each floor is 3 m, find: (i) the total vertical distance travelled, and (ii) their displacement from the starting point.Very short answer

(i) Up: ground → fourth floor = 4 floors = 4 × 3 = 12 m. Down: fourth → second floor = 2 floors = 2 × 3 = 6 m. Total distance = 12 + 6 = 18 m.

(ii) The student stops on the second floor, which is 2 floors above the ground floor, so the displacement = 2 × 3 = 6 m upwards.

Q3 A girl is riding her scooter and finds that its speedometer reading is constant. Is it possible for her scooter to be accelerating and if so, how?Short answer

Yes, it is possible. Speed can stay constant while the scooter is accelerating, provided the direction of motion is changing. Velocity is a vector, so a change in direction is a change in velocity, and any change of velocity means acceleration. So if she takes a turn or the road curves while the speedometer reading stays the same, the scooter is accelerating. This is exactly what happens in uniform circular motion, where the speed is constant but the direction of velocity changes continuously.

Q4 A car starts from rest and its velocity reaches 24 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find the average acceleration and the distance travelled in these 6 s.Numerical

u = 0 m s⁻¹, v = 24 m s⁻¹, t = 6 s.

Average acceleration a = (v − u) ÷ t = (24 − 0) ÷ 6 = 4 m s⁻².

Distance s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 4 × 6² = 2 × 36 = 72 m.

Check: v² = u² + 2as → 24² = 576 and 2 × 4 × 72 = 576. ✔

Q5 A motorbike moving with initial velocity 28 m s⁻¹ and constant acceleration stops after travelling 98 m. Find the acceleration of the motorbike and the time taken to come to a stop.Numerical

u = 28 m s⁻¹, v = 0 m s⁻¹, s = 98 m. Time is not needed for the first part, so use v² = u² + 2as.

0 = 28² + 2a(98) → 0 = 784 + 196a → a = −784 ÷ 196 = −4 m s⁻². The minus sign shows retardation — the acceleration acts opposite to the direction of motion.

Now use v = u + at: 0 = 28 + (−4)t → 4t = 28 → t = 7 s.

Q6 Figure 4.27 shows a position–time graph of two objects A and B that are moving along parallel tracks in the same direction. Do objects A and B ever have equal velocity? Justify your answer.Graph question

No. On a position–time graph the velocity is the slope of the line. Both graphs are straight lines, so both objects move with constant velocities — but the line for A is steeper than the line for B, so the slope (velocity) of A is greater than that of B. Since each velocity stays constant and the two constant values are different, A and B never have equal velocity at any instant.

Q7 A graph in Figure 4.28 shows the change in position with time for two objects A and B moving in a straight line from 0 to 10 seconds. Choose the correct option(s).
(i) The average velocity of both over the 10 s time interval is equal since they have the same initial and final positions.
(ii) The average speeds of both over the 10 s time interval are equal since both cover equal distance in equal time.
(iii) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is lower than that of B since it covers a shorter distance than B in 10 seconds.
(iv) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is greater than that of B since B's speed is lower than A's in some segments.Graph question

The correct options are (i) and (ii).

Why (i): both objects start at the same position at t = 0 s and finish at the same position at t = 10 s, so their displacements are equal. Equal displacement in the same time interval gives equal average velocity.

Why (ii): both move in a straight line in one direction without turning back, so for each of them the distance travelled equals the magnitude of the displacement. Since these are equal, the average speeds are equal too.

Why (iii) and (iv) are wrong: they are based on distances that the graph does not show to be different — both objects cover the same total path length in the 10 s.

Q8 A truck driver driving at the speed of 54 km h⁻¹ notices a road sign with a speed limit of 40 km h⁻¹ for trucks. He slows down to 36 km h⁻¹ in 36 s. What was the distance travelled by him during this time? Assume the acceleration to be constant while slowing down.Numerical

u = 54 km h⁻¹ = 54 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 15 m s⁻¹; v = 36 km h⁻¹ = 10 m s⁻¹; t = 36 s.

The acceleration is constant, so the average velocity is ½(u + v) = ½(15 + 10) = 12.5 m s⁻¹.

Distance s = average velocity × time = 12.5 × 36 = 450 m.

(Check with the equations: a = (10 − 15) ÷ 36 = −0.139 m s⁻², and s = ut + ½at² = 15 × 36 − ½ × 0.139 × 36² = 540 − 90 = 450 m. ✔)

Q9 A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 20 m s⁻¹ in 5 seconds. It then travels at 20 m s⁻¹ for 10 seconds and finally applies the brake (with uniform acceleration) to stop in 6 seconds. Find the total distance travelled.Numerical

The motion has three phases; find the distance in each and add.

Phase 1 (0 → 20 m s⁻¹ in 5 s): s₁ = ½(u + v)t = ½(0 + 20) × 5 = 50 m.

Phase 2 (constant 20 m s⁻¹ for 10 s): s₂ = v × t = 20 × 10 = 200 m.

Phase 3 (20 m s⁻¹ → 0 in 6 s): s₃ = ½(u + v)t = ½(20 + 0) × 6 = 60 m.

Total distance = 50 + 200 + 60 = 310 m.

Q10 A bus is travelling at 36 km h⁻¹ when the driver sees an obstacle 30 m ahead. The driver takes 0.5 seconds to react before pressing the brake. Once the brake is applied, the velocity of the bus reduces with constant acceleration of 2.5 m s⁻². Will the bus be able to stop before reaching the obstacle?Numerical · road safety

u = 36 km h⁻¹ = 10 m s⁻¹, a = −2.5 m s⁻², obstacle at 30 m.

Reaction distance (the bus keeps moving at 10 m s⁻¹ for 0.5 s) = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m.

Braking distance: using v² = u² + 2as with v = 0, 0 = 100 + 2(−2.5)s → 5s = 100 → s = 20 m.

Total stopping distance = 5 + 20 = 25 m, which is less than 30 m.

So yes — the bus will stop before the obstacle, with 5 m to spare. If the driver were distracted so that the reaction time doubled to 1 s, the stopping distance would become 10 + 20 = 30 m, and the bus would just touch the obstacle.

Q11 A student said, “The Earth moves around the Sun”. In this context, discuss whether an object kept on the Earth can be considered to be at rest.Discussion

Motion and rest are relative — they are always described with respect to a chosen reference point.

With respect to the Earth (say the ground or the room where the object is kept), the position of the object does not change with time, so it is at rest.

But with respect to the Sun, the same object is moving along with the Earth around the Sun, so it is in motion. Both statements are correct in their own frame of reference; neither is wrong. This shows that “at rest” is never an absolute statement.

Q12 The velocity–time graph from 0 s to 120 s for a cyclist is shown in Figure 4.30. Shade the areas (in different colours) representing the displacement of the cyclist (i) while the cyclist is moving with constant velocity, and (ii) when the velocity of the cyclist is decreasing. Also, calculate the displacement and average acceleration in the 120 s time interval.Graph question

The displacement is the area under the velocity–time graph. Break it into three parts.

(i) 0 s – 20 s, velocity increasing from 0 to 3 m s⁻¹: triangle, area = ½ × 20 × 3 = 30 m.

(ii) 20 s – 100 s, constant velocity 3 m s⁻¹: rectangle, area = 3 × 80 = 240 m — this is the part to shade for constant velocity.

(iii) 100 s – 120 s, velocity decreasing from 3 to 2 m s⁻¹: trapezium, area = ½(3 + 2) × 20 = 50 m — this is the part to shade for decreasing velocity.

Total displacement = 30 + 240 + 50 = 320 m.

Average acceleration = (v − u) ÷ t = (2 − 0) ÷ 120 = 0.0167 m s⁻² (≈ 1.7 × 10⁻² m s⁻²).

Q13 A girl is preparing for her first marathon by running on a straight road. She uses a smartwatch to calculate her running speed at different intervals. The graph (Figure 4.31) depicts her velocity versus time. Estimate the running distance based on the graph.Graph · estimation

The running distance is the area under the velocity–time graph. Read the graph in parts (velocity in km h⁻¹, time in hours).

0 – 1.5 h: velocity rises to about 7.5 km h⁻¹, so the average velocity is about 7.1 km h⁻¹ → distance ≈ 7.1 × 1.5 = 10.65 km.

1.5 h – 3 h: constant velocity 7.5 km h⁻¹ → 7.5 × 1.5 = 11.25 km.

3 h – 5.5 h: velocity falls from 7.5 to 6.5 km h⁻¹, average 7.0 km h⁻¹ → 7.0 × 2.5 = 17.5 km.

5.5 h – 7 h: constant 6.5 km h⁻¹ → 6.5 × 1.5 = 9.75 km.

Total distance = 10.65 + 11.25 + 17.5 + 9.75 = 49.15 km ≈ 49 km (about 50 km) — a sensible distance for a marathon training run.

Q14 On entering a state highway, a car continues to move with a constant velocity of 6 m s⁻¹ for 2 minutes and then accelerates with a constant acceleration 1 m s⁻² for 6 seconds. Find the displacement of the car on the state highway in the 2 min 6 s time interval by drawing a velocity–time graph for its motion.Graph · numerical

Phase 1: constant velocity 6 m s⁻¹ for 2 min = 120 s. This part of the graph is a horizontal line, and the displacement is the rectangle: s₁ = 6 × 120 = 720 m.

Phase 2: acceleration 1 m s⁻² for 6 s. The final velocity is v = u + at = 6 + 1 × 6 = 12 m s⁻¹, so this part of the graph is a rising straight line and the area is a trapezium: s₂ = ½(u + v)t = ½(6 + 12) × 6 = 54 m.

Total displacement = 720 + 54 = 774 m.

Q16 Rohan studies science from 6 PM to 7:30 PM at home. Consider the tip of the minute's hand of the wall clock. During the given time interval, what is its: (i) distance travelled, (ii) displacement, (iii) speed, and (iv) velocity? The length of the minute's hand is 7 cm.Numerical · circular motion

Time interval = 1.5 hours = 90 min = 5400 s. The minute hand makes 1 revolution per hour, so it makes 1.5 revolutions. Circumference = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm.

(i) Distance travelled = 1.5 × 44 = 66 cm.

(ii) At 6 PM the tip points to 12; after 1 full revolution (7 PM) it is back at 12, and in the next 30 min it reaches 6 — exactly opposite. So the tip is at the two ends of a diameter: displacement = 2r = 14 cm, directed from 12 towards 6 (vertically downward).

(iii) Speed = distance ÷ time = 66 ÷ 5400 = 0.0122 cm s⁻¹.

(iv) Velocity = displacement ÷ time = 14 ÷ 5400 = 0.0026 cm s⁻¹, directed from 12 to 6.

Think It Over (page 48)

The two questions that open the chapter, answered

1. How much distance should we maintain from the truck ahead to avoid a collision if it suddenly applies the brakes?

We must keep at least our own total stopping distance — the distance our vehicle travels from the moment the danger appears until it comes to rest. This has two parts: the reaction distance (the vehicle keeps moving at full speed while the driver reacts, u × reaction time) and the braking distance (u² ÷ 2a). For a car at 54 km h⁻¹ (15 m s⁻¹) with a reaction time of 0.5 s and a retardation of 4 m s⁻², that is 7.5 m + 28.1 m ≈ 36 m. In practice a gap of at least two to three seconds of travel time is recommended, because it grows automatically with speed.

2. Does this distance depend upon the speed with which we are moving?

Yes, strongly. The reaction distance grows in proportion to the speed, but the braking distance grows with the square of the speed, because s = u² ÷ 2a. So at double the speed the braking part grows with the square of the speed — and at 72 km h⁻¹ (20 m s⁻¹) the same car needs 10 m + 50 m = about 60 m instead of 36 m. This is why the safe gap must be increased sharply as speed rises, and further increased on wet roads or when the driver is tired.
