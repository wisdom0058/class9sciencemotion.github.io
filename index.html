Motion, and how we describe it

Everything in nature is in motion — a butterfly, a falling leaf, a train, the planets. Scientists study such a huge variety of motion by first looking at the simplest idealised forms: motion in a straight line, circular motion and oscillatory motion.

This chapter takes up motion in a straight line and uniform circular motion. You will learn five quantities — position, distance and displacement, average speed, average velocity and average acceleration — and you will learn to describe motion in three languages: words, numbers and equations, and graphs.

Along the way you will see why a driver must keep a safe distance from the vehicle ahead, why doubling your speed makes your braking distance four times longer, and why an object moving at a constant speed round a circle is still accelerating.
