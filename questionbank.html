② Section 2 — Assertion–Reason questions

Choose from: (A) both true, R explains A · (B) both true, R does not explain A · (C) A true, R false · (D) A false, R true.

Assertion (A): The displacement of an object can be zero even if it has covered a non-zero distance.
Reason (R): Displacement is the net change in position, which becomes zero if the object returns to its starting point.

Assertion (A): Average velocity can be negative, zero or positive.
Reason (R): Average speed has only a numerical value and no direction.

Assertion (A): A body moving with constant speed in a circle has an acceleration.
Reason (R): In circular motion the direction of the velocity changes continuously.

Assertion (A): The kinematic equations cannot be used if the acceleration is not constant.
Reason (R): These equations are derived assuming a single constant value of acceleration.

Assertion (A): If the speed of a vehicle is doubled, its braking distance becomes four times.
Reason (R): Braking distance is given by s = u² ÷ 2a.

Assertion (A): An object can be at rest for one observer and in motion for another.
Reason (R): Motion and rest are described with respect to a chosen reference point.

③ Section 3 — Very short answer questions

Q1 What is meant by describing the position of an object?

Specifying a fixed reference point and then giving the distance of the object from it together with the direction in which the object lies.

Q2 Under what condition is an object said to be in a state of rest?

When its position with respect to the chosen reference point does not change with time.

Q3 Define displacement.

Displacement is the net change in the position of an object between two given instants of time. Its magnitude is the distance between the two positions and its direction is from the first position towards the second.

Q4 State one difference between distance and displacement.

Distance is the total path length and needs only a numerical value, whereas displacement is the net change of position and needs both magnitude and direction.

Q5 Define average speed and give its SI unit.

Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ time interval. SI unit: m s⁻¹ (metre per second).

Q6 What is uniform motion in a straight line?

Motion in which the object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, for every choice of time interval, so its speed stays constant.

Q7 Define average velocity.

Average velocity = change in position (displacement) ÷ the time interval in which the change occurs.

Q8 When is the magnitude of average velocity equal to the average speed?

When the object moves along a straight line in one direction without turning back.

Q9 Define average acceleration and give its SI unit.

Average acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time interval; its SI unit is m s⁻² (metre per second squared).

Q10 When is the acceleration of a body opposite to the direction of its velocity?

When the magnitude of the velocity is decreasing, that is, when the body is slowing down (negative acceleration or retardation).

Q11 What is the value of the acceleration due to gravity near the Earth's surface?

g = 9.8 m s⁻², directed towards the centre of the Earth.

Q12 What does the slope of a position–time graph give?

The velocity of the object.

Q13 What does the slope of a velocity–time graph give, and what does the area under it give?

Slope gives acceleration; the area enclosed between the line and the time axis gives the displacement.

Q14 State the three kinematic equations.

v = u + at, s = ut + ½at² and v² = u² + 2as, valid for motion in a straight line with constant acceleration.

Q15 What is uniform circular motion?

Motion of an object along a circular path with constant speed.

Q16 Define the time period of circular motion.

The time taken by the object to complete one full revolution of the circular path; v = 2πR ÷ T.

Q17 In what direction does the velocity of a body in circular motion act?

Along the tangent to the circle at that point, in the direction of motion.

Q18 Why is uniform circular motion called accelerated motion?

Because the direction of the velocity changes continuously, and any change of velocity means acceleration.

④ Section 4 — Short answer questions

Q19 Explain with an example how the magnitude of displacement can be zero even when the distance travelled is not zero.

An athlete who runs one complete round of a circular track covers a distance equal to the circumference, yet the initial and final positions are the same. The net change in position is therefore zero, so the displacement is zero even though a large distance was travelled.

Q20 Explain how the average velocity of an object can be zero while its average speed is non-zero, using a daily-life example.

If you go from home to school and come back home along the same path, your displacement is zero, so the average velocity for the whole trip is zero. But you covered 2d of actual path, so the average speed = 2d ÷ t is non-zero.

Q21 Describe average acceleration. State its SI unit and when it acts opposite to the direction of motion.

Average acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time interval over which it occurs; SI unit m s⁻². It acts opposite to the direction of motion when the magnitude of the velocity is decreasing, for example when the brakes are applied to a moving vehicle.

Q22 Why must a driver maintain a safe distance from the vehicle ahead? Explain using negative acceleration and reaction time.

When a hazard appears, the driver takes a short reaction time before braking, during which the vehicle still moves at its full speed, and then it needs a braking distance u² ÷ 2a to stop. The safe distance must cover both, otherwise the vehicle will hit the one ahead if it brakes suddenly.

Q23 State the difference between average speed and average velocity with one example each.

Average speed uses the total distance travelled and is a scalar; average velocity uses the displacement and is a vector. A swimmer who swims 25 m and back in 50 s has an average speed of 1 m s⁻¹ but an average velocity of 0 m s⁻¹.

Q24 How can you tell from a position–time graph whether the object is at rest, moving uniformly, or accelerating?

A horizontal line means the object is at rest; a straight slanting line means uniform velocity (constant slope); a curved line means the velocity is changing, so the object is accelerating.

Q25 Distinguish between distance–time and velocity–time graphs.

On a distance–time graph the vertical quantity is distance and the slope gives speed; on a velocity–time graph the vertical quantity is velocity, the slope gives acceleration and the area under the line gives displacement.

Q26 A bus travels 40 km at 40 km h⁻¹ and then 60 km at 60 km h⁻¹. Find its average speed.

Total distance = 100 km, total time = 1 h + 1 h = 2 h, so the average speed = 100 ÷ 2 = 50 km h⁻¹.

Q27 What is the difference between speed and velocity?

Speed is the rate of covering distance and has no direction; velocity is the rate of change of position and includes direction. Speed is always positive, velocity can be negative or zero.

Q28 Explain why a body moving on a circular path at constant speed is accelerating, but a body moving on a straight road at constant speed is not.

On the circular path the direction of velocity keeps changing, so velocity changes and there is acceleration. On the straight road both the magnitude and the direction of velocity stay the same, so the acceleration is zero.

Q29 Write the uses of the three kinematic equations.

v = u + at gives the velocity after time t when acceleration is known (used when t is involved); s = ut + ½at² gives the displacement when time is known; v² = u² + 2as relates velocity and displacement when time is not given or needed.

Q30 Why does the distance travelled by a vehicle under braking grow four times when its speed is doubled?

Because the braking distance is s = u² ÷ 2a. Doubling u multiplies u² by four, so the braking distance becomes four times as large for the same retardation.

Q31 Draw/describe the velocity–time graph of a body moving with (i) constant velocity and (ii) constant acceleration.

(i) A straight line parallel to the time axis (zero slope, zero acceleration). (ii) A straight line slanting upwards from the initial velocity (constant positive slope, hence constant acceleration).

Q32 A body covers 4 km towards the north and then 3 km towards the east. Find its total distance and the magnitude of its displacement.

Total distance = 4 + 3 = 7 km. The two displacements are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the net displacement = √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5 km.

⑤ Section 5 — Long answer questions

Q33 (a) Differentiate between distance travelled and displacement.
(b) Under what specific condition is the magnitude of average velocity equal to the average speed?
(c) A boy walks 3 km east, then 4 km north in 2 hours. Calculate his total distance, the magnitude of his displacement, his average speed and the magnitude of his average velocity.

(a) Distance is the total path length covered; it has only a magnitude and is always positive. Displacement is the shortest straight-line change of position from the initial to the final point; it has both magnitude and direction and can be negative or zero. Distance is a scalar, displacement a vector.

(b) The two are equal in magnitude only when the object moves along a straight line in a single direction without turning back.

(c) Total distance = 3 + 4 = 7 km. The paths are perpendicular, so the displacement magnitude = √(3² + 4²) = 5 km. Average speed = 7 km ÷ 2 h = 3.5 km h⁻¹. Magnitude of average velocity = 5 km ÷ 2 h = 2.5 km h⁻¹.

Q34 (a) Define average speed, average velocity and average acceleration with their formulas.
(b) A bus moving at 36 km h⁻¹ accelerates for 10 s and reaches 54 km h⁻¹. Then the brakes are applied and it stops in 5 s. Find the average acceleration in both intervals.

(a) Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ time interval (scalar, m s⁻¹). Average velocity = displacement ÷ time interval (vector, m s⁻¹). Average acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time interval, a = (v − u) ÷ t (vector, m s⁻²).

(b) u = 36 km h⁻¹ = 10 m s⁻¹, v = 54 km h⁻¹ = 15 m s⁻¹, t = 10 s → a = 5 ÷ 10 = +0.5 m s⁻², in the direction of velocity. While braking: u = 15 m s⁻¹, v = 0, t = 5 s → a = −15 ÷ 5 = −3 m s⁻², opposite to the direction of velocity.

Q35 Explain, with a velocity–time graph, how (i) the acceleration and (ii) the displacement of a body moving with uniform acceleration can be obtained.

(i) The velocity–time graph of uniformly accelerated motion is a straight slanting line. The slope of this line, (v − u) ÷ t, equals the acceleration.

(ii) The area enclosed between the line and the time axis equals the displacement. For a line rising from u to v in time t, the region is a trapezium, so s = ½(u + v)t; using v = u + at this becomes s = ut + ½at².

Q36 Derive the three kinematic equations for motion in a straight line with constant acceleration.

v = u + at: from the definition a = (v − u) ÷ t, multiplying by t gives at = v − u, so v = u + at.

s = ut + ½at²: the displacement is the area under the velocity–time graph = rectangle (u × t) + triangle (½ × t × (v − u)) = ut + ½t(v − u). Substituting (v − u) = at gives s = ut + ½at².

v² = u² + 2as: from v = u + at, t = (v − u) ÷ a. Substituting in s = ut + ½at² and simplifying gives 2as = v² − u², that is v² = u² + 2as.

Q37 (a) What is uniform circular motion? (b) An athlete runs one complete round of a circular track of radius 7 m in 11 s. Find the distance travelled, the displacement, the average speed and the average velocity. (c) Why is this motion said to be accelerated?

(a) Motion along a circular path with constant speed.

(b) Distance = circumference = 2πR = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 m. After one full round the athlete is back at the starting point, so displacement = 0 m. Average speed = 44 ÷ 11 = 4 m s⁻¹. Average velocity = 0 ÷ 11 = 0 m s⁻¹.

(c) Because the direction of the velocity changes continuously, even though the speed stays constant — and a change of velocity means acceleration.

Q38 (a) Why is a safe distance needed between two vehicles? (b) A bus at 36 km h⁻¹ sees an obstacle 30 m ahead; the driver reacts for 0.5 s and then the bus decelerates at 2.5 m s⁻². Will it stop in time? (c) What happens to the stopping distance if the road is wet?

(a) Because the vehicle needs its full stopping distance = reaction distance + braking distance to come to rest, and keeping less than that can cause a collision.

(b) Reaction distance = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m; braking distance = u² ÷ 2a = 100 ÷ 5 = 20 m; total = 25 m < 30 m, so yes, the bus stops before the obstacle.

(c) On a wet road the grip is poorer, so the retardation a is smaller. Since the braking distance is u² ÷ 2a, a smaller a means a longer stopping distance — a safe gap must be increased.

⑥ Section 6 — Case-based questions

Q39 Riya's morning walk. Riya walks 300 m from her house to the park along a straight road, spends 5 minutes there and walks back home along the same road in 4 minutes. (a) What is the total distance she walked? (b) What is her displacement for the whole trip? (c) What is her average speed for the walking time? (d) Why is her average velocity zero?

(a) Distance = 300 m + 300 m = 600 m.

(b) She finishes at home, so the displacement = 0 m.

(c) Walking time = 4 min + 4 min = 8 min = 480 s (the 5 minutes at the park are not walking). Average speed = 600 ÷ 480 = 1.25 m s⁻¹.

(d) Because the net change in position is zero — the start and end points are the same — so average velocity = 0 ÷ 480 = 0 m s⁻¹.

Q40 Two cars on a highway. Car A moves with a constant velocity of 20 m s⁻¹ for 10 s. Car B starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m s⁻² for 10 s. (a) Find the velocity of car B after 10 s. (b) Find the distance covered by each car in 10 s. (c) Which car has the greater average velocity over the 10 s?

(a) v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 10 = 20 m s⁻¹.

(b) Car A: s = v × t = 20 × 10 = 200 m. Car B: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 100 = 100 m.

(c) Car A: average velocity = 200 ÷ 10 = 20 m s⁻¹. Car B: 100 ÷ 10 = 10 m s⁻¹. So car A has the greater average velocity, even though both end the 10 s with the same velocity of 20 m s⁻¹.

Q41 The cyclist's graph. A cyclist's velocity–time graph shows: velocity rising from 0 to 6 m s⁻¹ in the first 10 s, then constant at 6 m s⁻¹ for the next 20 s, then falling uniformly to 0 in the last 10 s. (a) Find the acceleration in each of the three parts. (b) Find the total distance travelled. (c) What is the average speed for the whole 40 s?

(a) Part 1: a = (6 − 0) ÷ 10 = 0.6 m s⁻². Part 2: constant velocity, so a = 0 m s⁻². Part 3: a = (0 − 6) ÷ 10 = −0.6 m s⁻².

(b) Distance = area under the graph = ½ × 10 × 6 + 6 × 20 + ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 + 120 + 30 = 180 m.

(c) Average speed = 180 ÷ 40 = 4.5 m s⁻¹.

Q42 Rohan's clock. Rohan studies from 6 PM to 7:30 PM. The minute hand of the wall clock is 7 cm long. (a) How many revolutions does the tip of the minute hand make? (b) What is the distance travelled by the tip? (c) What is its displacement? (d) Name the type of motion.

(a) The minute hand completes one revolution per hour, so in 1.5 hours it makes 1.5 revolutions.

(b) Circumference = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm, so the distance travelled = 1.5 × 44 = 66 cm.

(c) After 1.5 revolutions the tip is exactly opposite its starting position (12 → 6), so the displacement = diameter = 2r = 14 cm, directed from 12 towards 6.

(d) Uniform circular motion — constant speed along a circular path.

⑦ Section 7 — Numerical questions

Q43 A racing car has a uniform acceleration of 4 m s⁻². What distance will it cover in 10 s after starting?

u = 0, a = 4 m s⁻², t = 10 s. s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 4 × 100 = 200 m.

Q44 A car starting from rest attains a velocity of 24 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find (i) its acceleration and (ii) the distance travelled in these 6 s.

(i) a = (24 − 0) ÷ 6 = 4 m s⁻². (ii) s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 4 × 36 = 72 m (or s = ½(u + v)t = ½ × 24 × 6 = 72 m).

Q45 A motorbike moving at 28 m s⁻¹ stops after travelling 98 m. Find its acceleration and the time taken to stop.

v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 784 + 196a → a = −4 m s⁻² (retardation). Then v = u + at → 0 = 28 − 4t → t = 7 s.

Q46 A bus travelling at 36 km h⁻¹ is brought to rest in 4 s. Find its acceleration and the distance travelled before stopping.

u = 10 m s⁻¹, v = 0, t = 4 s. a = (0 − 10) ÷ 4 = −2.5 m s⁻². s = ½(u + v)t = ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 m.

Q47 A train moving at 72 km h⁻¹ slows down uniformly and stops in 200 m. Find its retardation and the time taken.

u = 20 m s⁻¹, v = 0, s = 200 m. From v² = u² + 2as: 0 = 400 + 400a → a = −1 m s⁻². From v = u + at: 0 = 20 − t → t = 20 s.

Q48 A body dropped from a height falls with an acceleration of 9.8 m s⁻². Find its velocity and the distance fallen after 3 s.

u = 0, a = 9.8 m s⁻², t = 3 s. v = u + at = 29.4 m s⁻¹. s = ut + ½at² = ½ × 9.8 × 9 = 44.1 m.

Q49 A car travelling at 15 m s⁻¹ accelerates uniformly at 2 m s⁻² for 5 s. Find its final velocity and the distance covered.

v = u + at = 15 + 10 = 25 m s⁻¹. s = ut + ½at² = (15 × 5) + (½ × 2 × 25) = 75 + 25 = 100 m.

Q50 The velocity of a scooter increases from 5 m s⁻¹ to 15 m s⁻¹ in 4 s. Find (i) the acceleration and (ii) the distance covered in this time.

(i) a = (15 − 5) ÷ 4 = 2.5 m s⁻². (ii) s = ½(u + v)t = ½ × 20 × 4 = 40 m.

Q51 A cyclist covers the first 30 m of a straight road in 5 s and the next 30 m in 3 s. Find his average speed for the whole 60 m.

Total distance = 60 m, total time = 8 s, so average speed = 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 m s⁻¹.

Q52 A car covers 100 km in 2 hours and then 120 km in 1.5 hours. Find the average speed of the car for the whole journey.

Total distance = 220 km, total time = 3.5 h, average speed = 220 ÷ 3.5 = 62.86 km h⁻¹ (≈ 17.5 m s⁻¹).

Q53 An object completes 4 revolutions of a circle of radius 3.5 m in 11 s. Find the distance travelled and the average speed.

Circumference = 2πR = 2 × (22/7) × 3.5 = 22 m. Distance = 4 × 22 = 88 m. Average speed = 88 ÷ 11 = 8 m s⁻¹.

Q54 A body thrown vertically upwards returns to the thrower's hand after 4 s. Taking g = 9.8 m s⁻², find (i) the initial velocity with which it was thrown and (ii) the maximum height reached.

(i) Upward journey takes 2 s to reach the top where v = 0: 0 = u − 9.8 × 2, so u = 19.6 m s⁻¹. (ii) Height = u² ÷ 2g = 19.6² ÷ (2 × 9.8) = 19.6 m.
