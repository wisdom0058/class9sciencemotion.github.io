Lesson · Velocity–Time Graphs

Topic 7 · two results from one graph

A velocity–time graph shows how the velocity of an object changes with time. It is plotted in exactly the same way as a position–time graph, but with velocity on the y-axis.

What the shape of the graph tells us

Straight line parallel to the time axis: the velocity is constant, so the acceleration is zero. Example: a car moving at a steady 20 m s⁻¹.

Straight line sloping upwards: the velocity increases by equal amounts in equal intervals of time — the acceleration is constant and in the direction of velocity.

Straight line sloping downwards: the velocity decreases by equal amounts in equal intervals of time — the acceleration is constant and opposite to the direction of velocity (negative acceleration).

Slope of the line gives acceleration

Take a part AB of the line. At time t₁ the velocity is u (point A) and at time t₂ it is v (point B). Then

acceleration = (v − u) ÷ (t₂ − t₁) = BC ÷ CA

which is exactly the slope of the line AB. For the car that speeds up, between 10 s and 20 s: a = (10 − 5) ÷ (20 − 10) = 5 ÷ 10 = 0.5 m s⁻². For the car that slows down the same calculation gives −0.5 m s⁻², the minus sign showing that the acceleration is opposite to the direction of velocity.

Area under the line gives displacement

Look at the region between the graph line and the time axis. Its area turns out to equal the displacement in that time interval, because velocity × time = displacement.

Constant velocity: the region is a rectangle. For a car at 20 m s⁻¹ for 6 s the displacement = 20 × 6 = 120 m.

Changing velocity: the region is a trapezium (or rectangle + triangle). For the car between 10 s and 20 s: area = rectangle ACDE + triangle ABC = (5 × 10) + (½ × 10 × 5) = 50 + 25 = 75 m.

Two results from a velocity–time graphslope = acceleration   ·   area = displacementFor a line rising from u to v in time t, the area is a trapezium: area = ½(u + v) × t.

Worked reading of the graph

Take the car whose velocity rises from 0 to 15 m s⁻¹ in 30 s (Tables 4.5).

(i) Acceleration = slope = (15 − 0) ÷ 30 = 0.5 m s⁻².

(ii) Displacement in 30 s = area of the triangle = ½ × base × height = ½ × 30 × 15 = 225 m.

(iii) Because the car keeps moving in one direction, the distance travelled is also 225 m.

Note: All the graphs in this chapter are for motion in a straight line in one direction only. In that special case the distance travelled equals the magnitude of the displacement, and the speed equals the magnitude of the velocity. If the position is zero at time zero, the position–time graph is the same as the distance–time graph, and the velocity–time graph is the same as the speed–time graph.

Ask and Understand

Slope, area and reading signs correctly.

Q1Why does the area under a velocity–time graph give the displacement?

Because displacement = velocity × time, and the area of a graph is also a product of the two quantities. For a constant velocity, the region is a rectangle whose area is height × base = velocity × time interval = displacement. When the velocity changes, we can imagine the interval divided into many tiny slices; in each slice the velocity is almost constant, so the small rectangle area is the small displacement, and adding all the slices gives the whole area. So the area equals the total displacement for the interval.

Q2How do we know whether the acceleration is positive or negative from the graph?

Look at the direction of the slope of the line. If the line rises to the right — velocity increasing — the slope is positive, so the acceleration is positive (in the direction of velocity) and the object is speeding up. If the line falls to the right — velocity decreasing — the slope is negative, so the acceleration is negative (opposite to the direction of velocity) and the object is slowing down. A horizontal line means zero slope, hence zero acceleration.

Q3What is the difference between a position–time graph and a velocity–time graph of the same motion?

They show the same motion in two different ways. On a position–time graph the vertical quantity is position, so the slope gives velocity and a straight line means constant velocity. On a velocity–time graph the vertical quantity is velocity, so the slope gives acceleration and the area gives displacement; here a straight line means constant acceleration. For uniformly accelerated motion the position–time graph is a curve (parabola) while the velocity–time graph is a straight slanting line.

Q4Can the area under a velocity–time graph ever be negative?

Yes, if the object moves in the negative direction. In that case the velocity is negative, the line lies below the time axis, and the area counted below the axis is taken as negative displacement. If the object moves forward and then comes back, the graph lies partly above and partly below the axis; the net displacement is found by subtracting the area below the axis from the area above it. Adding the two areas without signs would give the distance travelled instead.

Book Questions — Explained

1. Activity 4.4: Explain how the change in position and the change in time are obtained from a position–time graph, and how the velocity is then calculated. Activity 4.4

Take two points A and B on the graph. From A and B draw lines parallel to the axes to form the right-angled triangle ABC. The vertical side BC represents the change in position (s₂ − s₁), and the horizontal side CA represents the change in time (t₂ − t₁). Dividing BC by CA gives the average velocity: for the example, (80 − 40) ÷ (4 − 2) = 40 ÷ 2 = 20 m s⁻¹. This ratio BC/CA is the slope of the line AB.

2. Fig. 4.18(a): With a constant velocity of 20 m s⁻¹ for 6 s, what does the area OABC represent? Calculate it. Textbook, page 62

OA is the magnitude of the velocity and OC is the time interval, so the area of the rectangle OABC = velocity × time interval = displacement. The area = 20 m s⁻¹ × 6 s = 120 m, which is the displacement of the car from the origin in 6 seconds. This is why the area enclosed by a velocity–time graph and the time axis equals the displacement for that interval.

3. Fig. 4.18(b): Find the displacement of the object between 10 s and 20 s from the graph. Textbook, page 63

Take the points A at 10 s (5 m s⁻¹) and B at 20 s (10 m s⁻¹). The displacement is the area of ABDE, which is the rectangle ACDE plus the triangle ABC: (5 m s⁻¹ × 10 s) + (½ × 10 s × 5 m s⁻¹) = 50 m + 25 m = 75 m. The same value is obtained from the trapezium formula ½(u + v) × t = ½(5 + 10) × 10 = 75 m.
